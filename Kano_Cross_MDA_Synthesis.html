The central interpretation

The sector-wide rate should not be read as a typical agency experience. The weighted 23.9% result is shaped by a small number of very large institutions and by centrally paid personnel. At MDA level, results range from 0% to 92.1%; the median rises to 28.1% if the four zero-spend MDAs are excluded. The main management issue is therefore not simply “low” or “high” execution, but a combination of concentration, different component rhythms, abrupt quarter shifts and reporting/reconciliation exceptions.

What the figures say together
  • Coverage is complete, but activity is not broad-based.
  • Personnel is carrying Q2 growth; non-personnel execution weakened.
  • Large budgets and high burn rates are not the same: several small schools have the highest percentages.
  • Quarter attribution and blank monthly calendars remain important interpretation risks.

Cross-MDA findings and common trends

Six recurring patterns explain most of the variation in the attached report.

1

Expenditure is exceptionally concentrated

HMB, SMOH, PHCMB and KCHIMA account for 97.5% of all recorded H1 spending and 90.8% of the approved budget. HMB and SMOH alone account for 78.6% of expenditure.

2

Q2 growth is a personnel story

Personnel rises 202.7% from Q1 to Q2 and reaches a 39.9% H1 burn. Overhead falls 44.3% and capital falls 34.6%, so the aggregate Q2 improvement does not indicate across-the-board acceleration.

3

Capital dominates the budget, not the execution mix

Capital represents 61.4% of approved resources but 44.9% of H1 spending, with a 17.5% burn. Six of the eleven MDAs carrying capital lines record zero H1 capital expenditure.

4

Inactivity persists below the aggregate

PHIMA, School of Hygiene, School of Health Tech Bebeji and School of Health Tech Kano record zero H1 expenditure. Across all components, 10 of 35 lines are at zero.

5

Quarter patterns are polarised

HMB adds ₦10.90bn in Q2 and KCHIMA adds ₦4.26bn, while SMOH falls ₦5.81bn and PHCMB falls ₦2.55bn. These large counter-movements dominate the sector trajectory.

6

Reconciliation signals require care

Five MDAs contain reattributed figures. Two overhead lines have negative Q2 values, and two agencies have overhead activity in Q1 but nothing in Q2. These should be read as validation signals, not automatically as operational reversals.

Cross-MDA validation priorities

These questions follow directly from exceptions and common patterns visible in the attached HTML.

1. Separate “zero spending” from “not yet submitted”

Confirm the operational status of the four zero-H1 MDAs and the ten zero component lines. A confirmed zero and a missing return imply different follow-up actions.

2. Reconcile quarter attribution

Confirm the five MDAs with reattributed figures: SMOH, HMB, PHCMB, KCHIMA and KHETFUND, especially where the updated report moves activity between quarters.

3. Explain negative Q2 component values

HMB overhead and School of Basic Midwifery Gwarzo overhead are negative in Q2. Establish whether these are corrections, reversals or presentation effects.

4. Test whether non-personnel slowdown is timing or execution

Overhead and capital both decline in Q2. Review procurement, cash-release and reporting timing before interpreting the fall as a uniform performance deterioration.

5. Investigate high-burn small institutions

Dambatta (92.1%), Madobi (63.8%) and School of Nursing Kano (63.4%) are far above the sector rate. Confirm completeness of the approved denominator and whether the pace is sustainable.

6. Close the monthly evidence gap

Of 174 month-status cells across 29 non-personnel component calendars, 90 are blank and seven component calendars have no submissions at all. Use the official quarter totals without assuming every blank means zero.
